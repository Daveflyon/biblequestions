Marriage & Family

If the Bible says one wife, why did Old Testament men have many?

The Question

"You Christians say marriage is between one man and one woman. But the Bible is full of men with multiple wives. Abraham, Jacob, David, Solomon. Doesn't that contradict what you believe? If God wrote the rules, why did He let His own heroes break them?"

This is one of the most common challenges raised against the Christian view of marriage and it deserves a clear, honest answer. The short answer is this: God's design was always one man and one woman. What the Old Testament records is not God's approval of polygamy. It is God working with people where they were, while pointing toward His original intent.

1. God's Original Design

Before polygamy ever appears in Scripture, God sets the standard. At the very beginning of creation, before sin, before Moses, before any of the patriarchs, God establishes what marriage looks like:

"Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

Genesis 2:24

Notice the language carefully. Wife. Singular. One flesh. Two people joined as one. This is the blueprint and it comes straight from God before any human culture shaped the picture. Jesus himself quoted this exact verse to explain what marriage truly is (Matthew 19:5).

The starting point matters. The question is not "why did God allow many wives?" The better question is "why did some people depart from what God designed, and did God endorse that?" The answer, as we will see, is no.

2. Why Did Old Testament Men Have Many Wives?

This is where many people get confused and where a direct answer from Jesus himself is most helpful. When the Pharisees challenged him about marriage, Jesus gave a principle that applies equally to polygamy:

"Moses, because of the hardness of your hearts, permitted you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it was not so."

Matthew 19:8

The pattern Jesus reveals is clear. God sometimes permitted things He never designed, because of human stubbornness. He met people where they were in a fallen, broken world, while always pointing them back toward the original intention. Tolerance is not the same as approval.

This is consistent with how the whole Old Testament works. God dealt with slavery, violence and many other realities of the ancient world without stamping each one as righteous. He worked progressively through history to reveal His full purposes, which come to fullness in Christ.

What the Bible shows when men had many wives

Read the stories closely. Polygamy in Scripture never produces peace. It produces pain, almost without exception:

  • Jacob, Leah and Rachel (Genesis 29–30): Jealousy, rivalry, heartbreak. Two sisters competing for one man's love, using their servants as pawns.
  • David's household (2 Samuel 11–18): Adultery, murder, rape and a son leading a rebellion against his own father. The fruit of a divided household.
  • Solomon's 700 wives (1 Kings 11:3–4): "His wives turned away his heart." The wisest man who ever lived ended his life in idolatry because of his many wives. Scripture does not celebrate this. It mourns it.
Key point: The Bible records these stories. It does not commend them. There is a difference between the Bible describing what happened and the Bible prescribing what should happen. God is not endorsing the behaviour by including it in the record.

3. What the New Testament Says Clearly

If there was any ambiguity in the Old Testament, the New Testament removes it. Paul is direct:

"Each man should have his own wife, and each woman her own husband."

1 Corinthians 7:2

"A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife..."

1 Timothy 3:2

"If any man is above reproach, the husband of one wife..."

Titus 1:6

One wife. Singular. Stated plainly, repeatedly, in multiple letters to multiple churches.

The deeper reason: marriage mirrors Christ and the Church

Paul reveals why this matters at a level deeper than culture or law:

"Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her."

Ephesians 5:25

Christian marriage is not just a social arrangement. It is a living picture of the relationship between Jesus Christ and His people. Christ has one bride: the Church. That picture only makes sense with one husband and one wife, united and faithful. Polygamy does not just break a rule. It distorts the picture that marriage is meant to display.

4. How to Say It Simply

When someone raises this challenge in conversation, you do not need a theology degree to respond. Here is the core of the answer:

Plain Response

"God's design was always one man, one woman. He said so in Genesis, before any of those stories happened. The polygamy in the Old Testament was never commanded by God. It was tolerated, the way a parent might tolerate a child's bad habit while still guiding them toward better. Jesus explained this principle himself. God permitted certain things because people were hard-hearted, but that was never His intention. And if you look at the actual stories, Jacob, David, Solomon, none of it ended well. The New Testament makes the standard plain: one wife, one husband, a picture of Christ's faithful love for His one Church."

You do not need to be defensive. The question is a fair one and the Bible has a coherent answer. The Old Testament exceptions do not contradict God's design. They highlight why we need it.
