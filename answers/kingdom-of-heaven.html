The Kingdom

What is the kingdom of heaven?

The Question

"People talk about 'the kingdom of heaven' all the time. Isn't that just heaven, where you go when you die? And is it the same thing as the kingdom of God?"

Many people think of heaven after we die. That is true but incomplete. Jesus spoke about the kingdom more than almost anything else and He spoke about it as something that had already arrived. His first recorded message was this:

"The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand. Repent, and believe in the gospel."

Mark 1:15 (NKJV)

Core truth

Whether the Bible says "kingdom of heaven" or "kingdom of God", it points to God's rule: wherever God is King and His will is done. In Jesus that rule has come near. Anyone can enter it by turning to Him and trusting Him and one day it will fill the whole earth.

1. What It Is

Kingdom of heaven or kingdom of God?

Matthew mostly says "kingdom of heaven". Mark, Luke and John say "kingdom of God". Christians who love the Bible read this in two ways.

Same kingdom, two names

The Gospels often record the same saying with either phrase (Matthew 13:11; Mark 4:11; Luke 8:10). Matthew 19:23–24 uses both in back-to-back verses. Many Jewish readers said "heaven" as a respectful way of saying "God" (Daniel 4:26; Luke 15:18).

Two related ideas

Other teachers see a difference. For some, the kingdom of heaven is the place where God's throne is and the kingdom of God is His rule reaching out from there into our lives now (Psalm 103:19; Matthew 6:9–10). Others see the kingdom of heaven as the outward kingdom where good and bad grow together for now. The kingdom of God is then the inner reality of those who truly belong (Matthew 13:24–30; John 3:3).

Note: common ground

Both views agree on what matters most. God is King, His rule came near in Jesus and we enter by turning and trusting. The rest of this answer holds whichever view you take.

What a kingdom is

A kingdom has a king, a people, a rule (his word and will) and a realm. When the Bible speaks of God's kingdom, it speaks above all of His reign: God actually ruling. Jesus explained it in the prayer He taught His followers:

"Your kingdom come.Your will be doneOn earth as it is in heaven."

Matthew 6:10 (NKJV)

The two lines say the same thing twice. Where God's will is done, there His kingdom is. And God already rules over everything: "The Lord has established His throne in heaven, And His kingdom rules over all" (Psalm 103:19). So the question is not whether God is King. It is whether we live under His rule.

Not like the kingdoms of this world

The kingdoms we know help us see what is different about God's kingdom.

The Bible tells its own story about this. When Israel asked Samuel for a king they said, "Now make us a king to judge us like all the nations" (1 Samuel 8:5). God told Samuel, "they have not rejected you, but they have rejected Me, that I should not reign over them" (1 Samuel 8:7).

Standing before Pilate, Jesus said, "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36). It does not come from this world but it is very much for it.

Caution

This is not a call to look down on earthly government. Paul writes that "the authorities that exist are appointed by God" (Romans 13:1). The contrast is about where final authority sits.

The King who serves

Earthly rulers "lord it over" the people under them (Matthew 20:25). Jesus turned that upside down: "Yet it shall not be so among you; but whoever desires to become great among you, let him be your servant" (Matthew 20:26). Then He showed what He meant:

"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many."

Mark 10:45 (NKJV)

Compare two kings. Nebuchadnezzar looked over his city and said, "Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for a royal dwelling by my mighty power and for the honor of my majesty?" (Daniel 4:30). Jesus rode into Jerusalem "Lowly, and sitting on a donkey" (Matthew 21:5).

Going deeper

At the cross Jesus was crowned with thorns (John 19:2). Pilate's sign above Him read "JESUS OF NAZARETH, THE KING OF THE JEWS" (John 19:19). It was meant as mockery but it told the truth. This King wins by giving His life.

3. When and How

Here now and still to come

The kingdom is already here. When Jesus drove out evil He said, "But if I cast out demons by the Spirit of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you" (Matthew 12:28).

It is also still to come. That is why Jesus taught us to pray "Your kingdom come" (Matthew 6:10). The Bible ends with the whole world under the King:

"The kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ, and He shall reign forever and ever!"

Revelation 11:15 (NKJV)

Note

In Luke 17:21 the NKJV reads "the kingdom of God is within you" and its footnote gives "in your midst". Faithful translations are found with both readings. Jesus was speaking to Pharisees, so at the very least He meant that the King was standing in front of them.

How you enter

Jesus made the way in plain.

  • Turn and trust. "Repent, and believe in the gospel." (Mark 1:15)
  • A new start from God. "unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God." (John 3:3)
  • Come as a child. "unless you are converted and become as little children, you will by no means enter the kingdom of heaven." (Matthew 18:3)
  • God brings you in. "He has delivered us from the power of darkness and conveyed us into the kingdom of the Son of His love" (Colossians 1:13)

Those who enter belong to a new country: "For our citizenship is in heaven" (Philippians 3:20).

Living in it

Once you are in, the King's rule starts to shape everyday life. Paul sums it up: "for the kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit" (Romans 14:17). It is not first about outward rules. It is right living with God and others, peace that holds when life does not and joy that the Holy Spirit gives.

Practical tip

Jesus said, "But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you" (Matthew 6:33). In context "all these things" are food and clothing (Matthew 6:25–32). This week pray Matthew 6:10 slowly each day and ask God where His will needs to be done in your life.

4. Reflection

  1. What surprised you most about how the Bible describes the kingdom?
  2. How would you explain the kingdom of heaven to a friend in your own words? (Matthew 6:10)
  3. How is Jesus' way of being King different from the rulers you know? (Mark 10:45)
  4. What does the Bible say it takes to enter the kingdom and what does that mean for you? (Mark 1:15)
  5. Where in your life would you most want God's will to be done?
